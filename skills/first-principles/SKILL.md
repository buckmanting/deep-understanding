# First Principles

Build an explanation from the smallest set of true or accepted starting points needed for this learner and topic. The aim is a defensible chain of reasoning, not an artificial reduction of every subject to physics or axioms.

## Method

- Name the question precisely and distinguish the target concept from nearby terms.
- Identify the operative definitions, assumptions, constraints, and units. Mark assumptions as conventional, empirical, or simplifying when that distinction matters.
- Derive the key behavior in causal order. For mathematics, show intermediate reasoning and define symbols before relying on them. For social or empirical subjects, separate observed regularities from normative choices.
- Use one concrete case to test the reasoning, then state where the model stops applying or requires adjustment.

Keep foundational material proportional: use a familiar premise without re-explaining it when it is not the point of confusion. Do not use analogies as proof. If a premise is debated or uncertain, say so and route to `evidence-review` when evaluating its support is central.

## Handoff

Suggest `technical-deep-dive` when the learner needs operational detail beyond the derivation, and `knowledge-testing` when they want to verify they can reconstruct the reasoning independently.
